How? It uses the same Apple tool that businesses use to block certain apps on their employees’ phones.

There is no escape hatch like you have with Apple Screen Time and blocking apps.

Source Releases

Free

A switch. Plug in, pick apps, Apply. Only this Mac can unblock them — so unblocking means the cable.

Why it holds

Apple lets exactly one computer manage a supervised phone: the one that set it up. That's your Mac. The phone's own Settings has no remove button. So unblocking means the walk back to your laptop and the cable.

No account, no server. If we vanish, nothing changes.

Paid free during beta

A lock. Click Lock and nothing can unblock — not even your Mac — until the delay you chose runs out, or the person you chose says yes.

Why it holds

Lock does two things. It re-installs the block with a password you never see, and it moves your Mac's permission to manage the phone onto our server. After that, plugging into your Mac does nothing. The only way to unblock is the password, which iOS demands in Settings — and we hand it over only after the delay, or when your person approves. No Mac needed.

The one door that always works is erasing the phone. That's Apple's rule, and it's why nothing here can brick a phone.
